## Every way to get it runs 100% on your computer

Conversation Simulator is **free and open source**. The engine and the First
Words tutorial are Apache-2.0 and the official scenario packs are CC BY 4.0, so
you can always clone the [repository](https://github.com/outrightmental/ConversationSimulator),
build it, and run it at no cost — see the
[developer install guide](https://docs.conversationsimulator.com/dev/developer-install/).

The **$9.99 store editions** are that same software, packaged: signed, ready
to run, and kept up to date by the store. The Steam edition adds Steam Cloud
settings sync and achievements; the Microsoft Store and Mac App Store editions
are installed and updated by those stores (on the Mac App Store, voice is not
available yet). You are not paying to unlock the product — it is open in the source.
You are paying for a build you don't have to assemble yourself, and to fund
continued development. The local-first guarantee is identical either way: nothing
is transmitted to any server during play.

## Premium scenario packs

Beyond the free official packs, first-party **premium scenario-pack
expansions** — advanced professional skills, extended language courses, and themed
conversation bundles — are available as paid downloadable content on Steam. They
are optional add-ons on top of the free core; nothing that ships free is ever moved
behind the paywall.

## What to expect on first launch

No model ships in the box — you choose what runs on your machine. On first
launch the app walks you through downloading a starter model
(**Qwen3 4B Instruct**, ~2.5 GB, Apache-2.0), with the license and size shown
before anything is fetched. That first-run download (the model, plus the AI
engine itself in a source build) is the only time the app needs the internet.
After that, everything works offline.

**Recommended hardware:** any 64-bit machine with 8 GB RAM runs the starter
model; 6 GB+ of VRAM unlocks the larger, sharper models. Full details in the
[install guide](https://docs.conversationsimulator.com/start/install/) and
[local models guide](https://docs.conversationsimulator.com/play/local-models/).

## For developers

The whole platform is open source (Apache-2.0) on
[GitHub](https://github.com/outrightmental/ConversationSimulator) — see the
[developer install guide](https://docs.conversationsimulator.com/dev/developer-install/)
to run it from source and the
[contributing guide](https://docs.conversationsimulator.com/project/contributing/)
to get involved. Contributions to the open core are always welcome; the premium
expansion packs are developed separately and sold to sustain the project.

## Having trouble?

The [troubleshooting guide](https://docs.conversationsimulator.com/start/troubleshooting/)
covers the common failure modes, and
[GitHub issues](https://github.com/outrightmental/ConversationSimulator/issues)
are open to everyone. Beta builds include a one-click, fully-redacted
diagnostic report — nothing is ever uploaded automatically.
